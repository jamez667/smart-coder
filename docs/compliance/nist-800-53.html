Scope and limitations

DELIBERATELY NARROW. The moderate baseline selects roughly 300 controls. This pack addresses about a dozen — those with a genuine source-code component. Covered in part: SA-11 (developer testing), SA-15 (development process), SA-22 (unsupported components), SI-2 (flaw remediation), SI-10 (input validation), CM-2/CM-3 (baseline and change control), SC-8 (transmission confidentiality), SC-13 (cryptographic protection), SC-28 (protection at rest), IA-5 (authenticator management), AU-2 (event logging). NOT covered — entire families with no source surface: AT (awareness and training), CP (contingency planning), IR (incident response), MA (maintenance), MP (media protection), PE (physical and environmental), PS (personnel security), PM (programme management), RA (risk assessment, beyond dependency scanning), and most of AC (access control) and CA (assessment and authorization). An ATO requires a System Security Plan, an assessment by an independent assessor, and an authorizing official's risk acceptance. This pack contributes evidence to a handful of control implementations; it is not an assessment.

CA-2unknownControl assessments and authorization

Evidence to obtain

  • Plan of Action and Milestones (POA&M) actively tracking the remediation of assessment findings
  • Authorization to Operate (ATO) memo formally signed and dated by the designated Authorizing Official

Usually held by: Security Compliance Lead / Authorizing Official

What an auditor will probe: Show me the evidence that the Authorizing Official reviewed the residual risks in the latest assessment report before signing the ATO.

Obtain: Provide the System Security Plan, Security Assessment Report, and the ATO letter.

SA-22unknownUnsupported system components

Replace system components when support for the components is no longer available from the developer, vendor or manufacturer; or provide justification and documented approval for continued use.

all-of: worst of 2 check(s) is unknown

Evidence to obtain
  • Hardware and software inventory explicitly tracking vendor End-of-Life (EOL) and End-of-Support (EOS) dates
  • Signed risk acceptance memos or waivers from management for any currently deployed unsupported components
  • Migration project plans or ticketing system records demonstrating active replacement of EOL systems
