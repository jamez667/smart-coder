Scope and limitations

DELIBERATELY NARROW — and GDPR is the framework where that matters most. GDPR governs the lawfulness of personal data processing. This pack can evidence a small part of Article 32 (security of processing) as it appears in code. It cannot evidence anything about: * lawful basis and consent (Art. 6, 7) * data subject rights: access, rectification, erasure, portability (Art. 12-23) * records of processing activities (Art. 30) * data protection impact assessments (Art. 35) * the Data Protection Officer (Art. 37-39) * international transfers and their safeguards (Ch. V) * breach notification within 72 hours (Art. 33, 34) * processor contracts (Art. 28) Those are the substance of GDPR. A repository holds none of them. Article 32 itself requires measures "appropriate to the risk", which depends on what personal data is processed and how sensitive it is — questions this tool cannot answer. A passing result is engineering hygiene, not GDPR compliance, and must never be presented as the latter. Consult a data protection specialist.

Art5-6unknownPrinciples and lawfulness of processing

Personal data shall be processed lawfully, fairly and transparently; collected for specified purposes; adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary; accurate; kept no longer than necessary; and processed securely. Processing requires a lawful basis, and where that basis is consent it must be freely given, specific, informed and demonstrable.

all-of: worst of 1 check(s) is unknown

Evidence to obtain
  • Database logs or consent management platform (CMP) records showing user IDs, timestamps, and the exact consent language accepted
  • Legitimate Interest Assessments (LIAs) signed by legal counsel for processing without explicit consent
  • Automated data purge logs or closed Jira tickets demonstrating that data is actually deleted according to the retention schedule

Usually held by: Data Protection Officer (DPO) / Legal

What an auditor will probe: Show me the timestamped consent record for a specific user and the corresponding system logs proving their data was purged when the retention period expired.

Obtain: Provide the lawful basis analysis and, where relied on, consent records.

all-of: worst of 1 check(s) is unknown

Evidence to obtain
  • Data Subject Access Request (DSAR) ticketing queue showing request receipt dates and resolution dates
  • Redacted email or portal communications confirming fulfillment of rights to the data subject
  • Checklists or system query logs proving data was successfully deleted from primary databases, third-party SaaS vendors, and backups

Obtain: Provide the DSAR procedure and evidence of requests handled within statutory deadlines.

Art33-34unknownPersonal data breach notification

Notify the supervisory authority of a personal data breach without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours of becoming aware of it; communicate to data subjects where the breach is likely to result in high risk.

all-of: worst of 1 check(s) is unknown

Evidence to obtain
  • The internal breach register detailing the timeline of discovery, risk assessment, and resolution for past incidents
  • Incident response tickets (e.g., PagerDuty, Jira) showing the exact time the security team became aware of an event
  • Copies of actual notifications sent to the Supervisory Authority and data subjects, or post-mortem reports from tabletop exercises if no breaches occurred

What an auditor will probe: Provide the incident timeline for a recent breach or tabletop exercise to prove the supervisory authority was notified within the strict 72-hour window from the moment of discovery.

Obtain: Provide the breach response procedure and the breach register.
