Scope and limitations

This pack inspects source-controlled artifacts only. It cannot observe running infrastructure, cloud provider configuration, identity provider settings, HR processes, vendor agreements, or incident records. Controls depending on those are reported Unknown by design and appear in the manual-evidence worklist. Roughly five of the thirty-three common-criteria points yield deterministic source evidence. A passing result here is evidence toward a control, never an attestation of compliance.

CC6.1gapLogical access — credentials are not committed to source

The entity implements logical access security software, infrastructure, and architectures over protected information assets to protect them from security events. Credentials committed to version control defeat every downstream access control, because the repository history retains them after deletion.

all-of: worst of 5 check(s) is gap

Remediation: Rotate any exposed credential immediately — deleting the file is not sufficient, as the value remains in git history. Then add the path to .gitignore and adopt a secret scanner as a pre-commit hook.

CC7.2gapSystem monitoring for anomalies

The entity monitors system components and the operation of those components for anomalies indicative of malicious acts, natural disasters, and errors affecting the entity's ability to meet its objectives.

majority: 1 pass / 1 gap of 3 check(s)

Remediation: Adopt structured logging with a correlation id, and route alerts to an on-call destination. Evidence of the alert routing usually lives outside the repo.

CC7.1unknownVulnerability detection in dependencies

The entity uses detection and monitoring procedures to identify changes to configurations that introduce new vulnerabilities, and susceptibilities to newly discovered vulnerabilities.

any-of: no mechanism evidenced, and 2 of 2 check(s) were indeterminate

Evidence to obtain
  • Historical scan reports from the automated dependency scanning tool (e.g., Snyk, Dependabot)
  • Ticket history (e.g., Jira) showing the triage, assignment, and resolution of specific dependency vulnerabilities
  • CI/CD pipeline execution logs demonstrating that builds are blocked when critical vulnerabilities are detected

Usually held by: DevSecOps / Engineering

What an auditor will probe: Trace a recently discovered critical dependency vulnerability from the initial automated alert through to the deployed patch, proving it was resolved within your policy's SLA.

Obtain: Add a dependency audit step (cargo audit, pip-audit, npm audit, Dependabot) to CI.

CC6.1-LOCALunknownLocal secret hygiene — untracked credentials in the working tree

Secrets present in a working directory but excluded from version control are not committed, and are therefore outside the strict scope of CC6.1. They remain an exposure on developer machines and in backups, and are one forced add away from entering history permanently.

all-of: worst of 1 check(s) is unknown

Evidence to obtain
  • MDM (Mobile Device Management) reports showing full disk encryption enabled on all developer workstations
  • Configuration logs of enforced pre-commit hooks or local secret scanners (e.g., GitLeaks, Talisman) on developer machines
  • Signed Acceptable Use Policy acknowledgments covering the handling of local cryptographic keys and secrets

Usually held by: IT / Engineering Lead

What an auditor will probe: How do you verify that developers are not bypassing local secret scanners or syncing local keys to unapproved personal cloud backups?

Obtain: Prefer a managed secret store or an OS keychain over a file on disk. Where a local file is unavoidable, keep it gitignored (as here), restrict its permissions, and rotate on any suspicion of exposure.

CC1.1unknownControl environment — commitment to integrity and ethical values

The entity demonstrates a commitment to integrity and ethical values. This is an organizational control: board oversight, tone at the top, and a code of conduct whose operation is tested through interview and observation, not code review.

all-of: worst of 1 check(s) is unknown

Evidence to obtain
  • Dated signature logs from the HR system showing all employees acknowledged the Code of Conduct upon hire and annually
  • Board of Directors or executive committee meeting minutes demonstrating active discussion of security, ethics, and compliance
  • Operating logs from the whistleblower hotline or HR incident tracking system

Usually held by: HR / Legal

What an auditor will probe: Beyond having a Code of Conduct, how does executive management actively demonstrate and enforce these values when an ethical or security violation is actually reported?

Obtain: Provide the code of conduct, its acknowledgement records, and evidence of board oversight.

CC2.3unknownCommunication with external parties

The entity communicates with external parties regarding matters affecting the functioning of internal control, including a channel for reporting security issues.

all-of: worst of 1 check(s) is unknown

Evidence to obtain
  • Ticket history from the external security reporting alias (e.g., security@ inbox or HackerOne) showing triage, internal routing, and response
  • Incident response logs that were explicitly triggered by an external vulnerability report
  • Published Vulnerability Disclosure Program (VDP) guidelines or a live security.txt file

Usually held by: Security Team

What an auditor will probe: Show me the lifecycle of an external security report from the moment it hits your public inbox to the final communication sent back to the external researcher.

Obtain: Provide the vulnerability disclosure policy and evidence of its use.
