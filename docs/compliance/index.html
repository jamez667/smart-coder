We scanned the smart-coder source code against 10 compliance frameworks, assessing 111 controls. The automated tool verified 43 controls as passed and identified 6 gaps. The remaining 62 controls are unknown, meaning they require organizational or documentary evidence outside the scope of a source code scan rather than representing a failure. The overall determinacy of this scan was 44 percent, with a pass rate of 39 percent of assessed controls. There were 0 tool errors, and the command-exit-code capability was disabled for this assessment.

Addressing the identified gaps offers significant leverage across multiple standards. The highest priority is implementing automated dependency vulnerability scanning for acquired components, which resolves a high-severity gap shared across the NIST Secure Software Development Framework, PCI DSS v4.0, and NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5. Establishing signed releases and build provenance in the release pipeline will resolve a second high-severity gap present in both the NIST SSDF and SLSA v1.0 frameworks.

The tool's visibility varies depending on the nature of the standard. The EU regulatory cluster, including NIS2, DORA, and the AI Act, showed the lowest determinacy at 20 percent, as these frameworks largely rely on governance controls requiring documentary evidence rather than source-visible technical configurations.

How to read this

Determinacy is the number that matters. It is the share of in-scope controls the tool could decide either way. A low figure does not mean poor security — it means most of that framework is organizational (board oversight, vendor contracts, training records, incident procedures) and simply is not visible in source code.

Each framework's page states, in its own scope note, exactly what it cannot assess. That honesty is the point: a report claiming broad coverage of a governance framework from a code scan would be misleading.
