This automated source-code scan assessed the smart-coder project against 111 controls across 10 frameworks with zero tool errors, verifying 37 passed controls and identifying 10 gaps. The tool marked 64 controls as unknown because they cannot be determined from source code. Looking at where evidence physically lives, the code domain passed 22 of 36 controls, with 5 gaps and 9 unknowns, reaching 75 percent determinacy. Infrastructure passed 13 of 37 controls, with 5 gaps and 19 unknowns at 49 percent determinacy. Documentation showed 2 passed controls, zero gaps, and 1 unknown out of 3. The organizational domain contains 35 controls, all unknown. This zero percent determinacy is not a shortfall; it simply reflects that this evidence lives in HR systems, contracts, and board minutes rather than source files.

Resolving specific gaps will satisfy requirements across multiple frameworks simultaneously. The highest leverage items are implementing automated dependency vulnerability scanning and ensuring application logging is present. Both are high-severity gaps spanning three frameworks each, including NIST SSDF, PCI DSS v4.0, NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5, CIS v8, and HIPAA. Adding signed releases and build provenance will resolve a high-severity gap shared by NIST SSDF and SLSA v1.0. Frameworks relying heavily on governance, such as the EU regulatory cluster of NIS2, DORA, and the AI Act, showed the lowest determinacy at 20 percent because they require documentary evidence outside this repository.

How to read this

Determinacy is the number that matters. It is the share of in-scope controls the tool could decide either way. A low figure does not mean poor security — it means most of that framework is organizational (board oversight, vendor contracts, training records, incident procedures) and simply is not visible in source code.

Each framework's page states, in its own scope note, exactly what it cannot assess. That honesty is the point: a report claiming broad coverage of a governance framework from a code scan would be misleading.
